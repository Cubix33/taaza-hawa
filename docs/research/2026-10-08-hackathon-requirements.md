# Environmental Hacks: requirements for evaluating enhancements

Verified 8 October 2026 against the organiser's current pages. `thishack.md` supplies local context; the linked official pages are authoritative.

## Track and judging

- Select the track that best fits the project; tracks are judged independently. Air explicitly includes AQI, exposure, stubble burning, indoor air and school safety. Monitoring, warnings and useful actions fit. [Overview and FAQ](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/env)
- Criteria are idea/impact, AWS use, usability, execution and the recorded demo. The organiser favours a specific solved problem and functioning features over broad scope. No numerical weights are published on this page. [Judging](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/env#judging)
- Prize eligibility requires at least one AWS open-source tool or AWS deployment; local and deployed approaches receive equal consideration. Additional tools are allowed. [Build approaches](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/env)

## Originality and submission rules

- Build during the event. Pre-event learning/planning/practice is allowed; previously started projects are ineligible even after a rewrite. [Rules, section 03](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/env/rules)
- Libraries, public APIs, boilerplate and templates are allowed; judging concerns event additions. Other authors' work needs credit and a compatible licence. Disclose AI coding tools. [Rules, section 03](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/env/rules)
- Copying, misrepresenting an old project, or repository history inconsistent with event dates disqualifies the team. [Rules, section 05](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/env/rules)
- Submit a public repository, short problem/build/AWS writeup and YouTube video under three minutes. Show AWS use and working features: claims only in the writeup do not count. [Rules, sections 03–04](https://www.wemakedevs.org/aws/env/rules)

## Implications for Taaza Hawa (our judgement)

Keep enhancements tied to the existing decision and evidence loop: trustworthy inputs, understandable uncertainty, actionable exposure advice and independently checked predictions. A generic validation technique does not require adopting a competitor's product identity; implement from the project's own needs and data constraints, with attribution where appropriate.

Reject a seven-project feature bundle. Heat physiology, pulmonary simulations, regulatory compliance, fire attribution and civic escalation each introduce another product promise and proof burden. The rules do not forbid related ideas or explicitly ban cross-track features; the argument for avoiding them is focus, execution and originality, not an invented eligibility rule. Independent design and honest attribution matter more than renaming borrowed features.
