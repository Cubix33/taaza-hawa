## Q1. Hotspot "local excess" and dust-suppression effect sizes

- **Hotspot definition (DPCC 2018):** annual PM10 > 300 and PM2.5 > 100 µg/m³ within 2 km of a station; 13 hotspots. REPORTED. CPCB's daily bulletin names "Hotspots for today" — 8 Oct 2024: Anand Vihar, Mundka, Jahangirpuri, Vivek Vihar, Sonia Vihar. VERIFIED (cpcb.gov.in/uploads/AQM/AQ-NCR-08102024.pdf).
- **Magnitude of excess:** CSE winter 2024-25: Jahangirpuri 201, Anand Vihar 196, Wazirpur/Mundka 185 vs city 174 µg/m³ (+6 to +15 %); CSE says local build-up can reach +60 % over citywide means. REPORTED (cseindia.org megacities note, Mar 2025). Annual 2024: Jahangirpuri 119, Bawana/Wazirpur 113, Anand Vihar 111 vs 104.7 (+6 to +14 %). VERIFIED (cseindia.org note 12956).
- **Transient hotspots:** Bhardwaj et al., 28 low-cost sensors + CPCB, May 2018–Nov 2020: 660 public-network hotspots + 189 hidden; Gaussian plume explains 65 % of *transient* hotspots (local sources). No µg/m³ excess in abstract. VERIFIED (arxiv.org/abs/2410.04309).
- **Traffic signature:** CSE Oct–Nov 2025: PM2.5 tracks NO2 at 7–10 and 18–21 h; Dwarka Sec-8 broke the CO 8-h standard on 55 of 59 days. VERIFIED (CSE 12956).
- **Anti-smog gun, Delhi:** Dec 2017 Anand Vihar trial — hourly PM2.5 444→421→476→509→460, PM10 630→842 µg/m³ during the 10–14 h trial: no detectable drop; CSE: effect "limited by space and time." REPORTED (Business Standard, Dec 2017). Govt 2023 bio-enzyme spray claim: −55 % PM2.5/−32 % PM10, rebounding +62 %/+51 % within 3 h; unreviewed. REPORTED (Deccan Herald).
- **Best sprinkling study (Baoding, China, TRAKER):** road-dust PM −37 to −70 % by road class; **effective time ≈ 62 min**, baseline restored ~72 min after spraying stops. REPORTED (ScienceDirect S1001074221005234). Construction sprinklers −72 % PM2.5 at source. REPORTED (PMC10979197).
- **Implication:** sprinkling suppresses resuspended PM10 for ~1 h within tens of metres; no Delhi evidence of a PM2.5 change at any CAAQMS monitor.

## Q2. Open-burning signatures in hourly station data; burning share

- **Night BC excess:** Delhi aethalometer study (AAQR 2022, 10.4209/aaqr.220128): night BC 6.01 ± 5.8 vs day 3.99 ± 3.42 µg/m³; BC–PM2.5 r = 0.78, BC–CO r = 0.46. REPORTED (abstract only).
- **Day/night marker:** in burning-dominated areas the day–night PM2.5 difference exceeds the CO difference (open fires are PM-rich, CO-poorer than industry). REPORTED (npj 2024, s41612-024-00836-6).
- **Bhalswa landfill fire 2022:** nearby PM2.5 +45–55 %, max ~107 µg/m³ average. REPORTED (10.1007/s13762-023-04921-w).
- **Shares:** TERI/ARAI 2018 winter Delhi PM2.5: transport 28 %, industry 30 %, dust 17 %, biomass (residential + field) 14 %; 22 % for Delhi+NCR towns. REPORTED (teriin.org exec summary). IIT Kanpur 2016: biomass 24 %, SIA 32 %, vehicles 17 %. REPORTED. UrbanEmissions: waste burning ≈ 5,300 t/yr ≈ 8 % of PM2.5 emissions. REPORTED. The 20–24 h city-wide PM2.5 peak is boundary-layer driven, not only burning (Sharma 2026, Q6).
- **Engine caveat:** CPCB gives hourly PM2.5/PM10/CO/NO2 but **no BC**. Defensible flag: single-station PM2.5 jump with neighbours flat, CO co-rise, low wind. The *spatial* difference discriminates; the night rise alone does not.

Long-term (Table 6-1, m³/day mean): 6–<11 y 12.0; 11–<16 y 15.2; 21–<31 y 15.7; 31–<41 y 16.0. VERIFIED.

- **Dose arithmetic:** dose = C × IR × t. Moderate → sedentary cuts IR **~5× (adult 2.6e-2 → 4.2e-3)**, ~4.6× for ages 6–11. 60 min moderate at 150 µg/m³ = 234 µg inhaled (adult) vs 38 µg seated.
- **Indoor/outdoor PM2.5, Delhi:** Smart Air multi-site indoor ≈ 68 % of outdoor. REPORTED. Residential dry-season I/O 0.97 ± 0.14 (146 vs 153 µg/m³): naturally ventilated homes give almost no protection. REPORTED (s11869-013-0212-0). Full AC roughly halves infiltration; NV spaces 2–5× mechanically ventilated ones. REPORTED. Filtered-ventilation Delhi school: 7 vs 142 µg/m³ (−95 %); classroom HEPA alone −57 %. VERIFIED (REHVA Journal). Delhi apartment purifier test (PLOS ONE 0167999): efficacy falls as outdoor pollution rises; no % given. VERIFIED.
- **"Go indoors" effect:** NV room −3 to −30 %; AC/closed ≈ −50 %; purifier −57 to −95 %. With the IR drop, a child moved from moderate outdoor play to seated in an AC room cuts dose ~9×.
- **Behaviour change:** Bogotá RCT (Blackman & Hoffmann, Land Economics 2025): AQ-app training raised knowledge and *self-reported* avoidance; exposure not measured. VERIFIED. Scotland personal-exposure-feedback RCT (JESEE 2024, s41370-024-00661-2): N = 28 (17 analysable); at-home PM2.5 −5.7 vs −4.7 µg/m³ control (n.s.); targeted behaviours −43.9 µg/m³; asthma symptom-hours −4.6 % vs +0.5 %; NNT 5. VERIFIED. Australian alert-message RCT raised *intentions* only. REPORTED (Env Int 2019). **No RCT shows activity-aware feedback cuts measured dose; none in India.**

## Q4. Rooftop PV soiling from yield data

- **RdTools `soiling_srr`:** inputs = daily insolation-weighted normalized energy + daily POA insolation; defaults `day_scale` 13, `min_interval_length` 7 d, `clean_threshold` 'infer', 1000 reps, 68.2 % CI. No formal minimum length, but sub-7-day intervals are discarded, so **weeks-to-months of daily data** are needed; degradation needs ≥ 2 yr. VERIFIED (rdtools.readthedocs.io). CODS targets multi-year series. REPORTED.
- **Delhi soiling rates:** Cleantech/MIT/SERIS Delhi test (dry months 2015–16): **0.3–0.6 %/day**, PR gain on cleaning 27–40 %; haze cut irradiance 20–40 % on bad days, ~6 % annually. VERIFIED (renewablewatch.in on 32nd EU PVSEC paper). Gujarat 0.37 %/day; a "Delhi 2017: 0.16 %/day" figure is snippet-only. UNVERIFIED.
- **Haze loss:** Peters et al. 2018 (EES 10.1039/c8ee01100a): Delhi PV insolation **−11.5 ± 1.5 % (≈200 kWh/m²/yr)** 2016–17, with an empirical insolation–PM2.5 relation. VERIFIED. Bergin et al. 2017 (EST Lett 10.1021/acs.estlett.7b00197, Gandhinagar): ~50 % output jump on cleaning after weeks; deposit 92 % dust by mass, anthropogenic PM ~equal optically. REPORTED.
- **Dust vs haze:** Ghosh, Dey et al. 2022 (IIT Delhi, ERL 10.1088/1748-9326/ac5d9a): attenuation via Beer's law on AOD, soiling via a soiling optical depth from deposition fluxes; India 2001–18: 0.56 kWh/m²/day attenuation vs 0.59 with soiling; 12 % (horizontal) to 41 % (dual-axis) loss. VERIFIED. **No paper separates the two from one rooftop's yield + public PM2.5 alone.** Defensible version: normalise yield by pvlib clear-sky POA, regress residual on same-day PM2.5 (haze) vs cumulative dry days (soiling sawtooth, rain resets).

## Q5. Classrooms: CO2 and PM2.5 mass balance

- **Measured classrooms:** Goyal & Khare (IIT Delhi, EMA 10.1007/s10661-010-1600-7), NV Delhi school by a road: indoor PM10/2.5/1 **above** outdoor in school hours; one-box mass balance R² 0.87–0.90. REPORTED. Bilaspur govt schools indoor PM2.5 mean 281.67 µg/m³. REPORTED (PMC11302538). Indian NV classrooms 15.6–61.5 L/s per person. REPORTED. Delhi filtered school 1.5 L/s·m² ≈ 2 ACH. VERIFIED (REHVA).
- **CO2 generation (Persily & de Jonge 2017, Indoor Air, Table 4, L/s at 273 K):** boys 6–<11: 0.0025 (1.0 met), 0.0030 (1.2), 0.0035 (1.4), 0.0040 (1.6); girls 6–<11: 0.0023/0.0027/0.0032/0.0037; 11–<16 boys 0.0034/0.0041/0.0048/0.0054, girls 0.0029/0.0035/0.0041/0.0047; adults 21–30 M 0.0039/0.0048, F 0.0031/0.0038. Formula VCO2 = RQ × BMR × M × 0.000569 (BMR MJ/day). VERIFIED (NIST PDF).
- **Mass balance:** dC/dt = λ(P·Cout − C) − k·C + S/V; P ≈ 0.8–1.0 with open windows; PM2.5 deposition k ≈ 0.2–0.5 h⁻¹ (Thatcher & Layton 1995; Long et al. 2001). REPORTED (not re-fetched). CO2 steady state: Cin = Cout + G·n/(λV).
- **Smog trade-off:** windows open → CO2 falls, PM2.5 → outdoor; closed → 40 kids × 0.003 L/s = 0.12 L/s ≈ 430 L/h into 200 m³ → +2,150 ppm/h unventilated. No Indian study quantifies the optimum; it is a modelled trade-off.

VERIFIED (CREA). 8 Oct 2025 AQI 81 Satisfactory (O3/CO), 9 Oct AQI 93; surge 18–25 Oct; Oct 2025 mean AQI 232 vs 226 (2024), 208 (2023). REPORTED (CAQM snippet); monthly means VERIFIED (aqi.in).

## Q7. Stubble transport and the evening-fire shift

- **Travel time:** HYSPLIT trajectories from burn zones reach Delhi in **14–22 h, within 36 h**; 1–3 day plume lags in a 30-site sensor network (Nov 2022 plumes > 500 µg/m³). REPORTED. Kotrike & Sridhar 2026 (AQAH 10.1007/s11869-026-01885-x): fire counts explain 78 % of Delhi AOD variance, Oct–Nov 2020–24. VERIFIED abstract.
- **Weak coupling:** Mangaraj & Patra (npj Clim Atmos Sci 2025, s41612-025-00901-8): PM2.5 vs fire counts r = 0.35 (Punjab), 0.65 (Haryana); embassy PM2.5 −24 % while fires −49 %/−72 %; WRF-CMAQ share Punjab ~14 %, Haryana ~44 % (double-counting likely), Oct–Nov 2022. VERIFIED.
- **IITM DSS daily share:** 12–21 Oct 2024 mean **0.92 %**; Oct–Nov 2025 mean 4.2 %, peak > 22 % on 12–13 Nov 2025, Dec 2025 0.2 %. VERIFIED (CSE 31 Dec 2025; DTE). Oct 2024 < 10 %. VERIFIED (CREA).
- **Evening-fire shift:** iFOREST report (9 Dec 2025, Meteosat SEVIRI + Sentinel-2): **> 90 % of large Punjab fires after 15:00 in 2024–25 vs 3 % in 2021**; MODIS/VIIRS see 10:30–13:30, so counts fell > 90 % while burnt area fell far less. VERIFIED (Tribune). NASA EO (15 Dec 2025, Jethva/Gupta, GEO-KOMPSAT-2A): peak fire hour 13:30 (2020) → 17:00 (2024) → 16–18 h (2025). VERIFIED. Fire-count-driven DSS shares are therefore biased low in 2024–25.

## Deterministic engines you can defend

| Engine | Inputs | Formula / standard | Known error |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inhaled dose | CPCB hourly PM2.5 (nearest station), activity class, duration, age | Dose = Σ C·IR·Δt; IR from EPA EFH Table 6-2 (VERIFIED) | IR 95th/mean ≈ 1.4×; station-to-person spatial error ±30–60 % (Smart Air I/O 0.68, Bhardwaj hidden hotspots) |
| Indoor-vs-outdoor dose choice | Outdoor C, room type (NV/AC/purifier) | C_in = F_inf·C_out, F_inf ∈ {0.7–1.0 NV, ~0.5 AC, 0.05–0.4 purifier} | F_inf is a literature band, not measured for the user's room |
| Classroom CO2/PM2.5 balance | n pupils, age, met, V, λ, C_out | Persily–de Jonge G (VERIFIED); dC/dt = λ(P·C_out − C) − kC + G/V; k 0.2–0.5 h⁻¹ | λ unknown without a CO2 logger (±50 %); k band ±2× |
| Hotspot local excess | Hourly PM2.5/PM10 all stations | Excess = C_station − median(all stations) per hour; flag if > 2·MAD for ≥ 2 h | Sensor drift/outages; meteorology shared city-wide cancels in the median |
| Burning-event flag | Station PM2.5, CO, PM10, wind | Local excess + PM2.5/PM10 ratio rise + CO co-rise, night, low wind | No BC at CPCB; false positives from fireworks/traffic; precision unknown until validated |
| PV soiling / haze split | Daily kWh, pvlib clear-sky POA, rain, CPCB PM2.5 | RdTools SRR (≥ 7-day intervals); residual ~ PM2.5 (haze, Peters −11.5 %/yr) + dry-day count (soiling 0.3–0.6 %/day) | Collinear in dry smog weeks; needs ≥ 1 cleaning/rain cycle to separate |
| Stubble influence index | NASA FIRMS counts, HYSPLIT/wind, IITM DSS share | Lag 14–36 h; share = DSS value (report as range) | Counts biased low > 90 % after 2024 evening shift; DSS share itself model-based |

## Claims you must NOT make

1. "Sprinkling/anti-smog guns reduce PM2.5 at a monitor" — Delhi's public trial showed no drop; Chinese evidence is PM10, ~1 h, metres-scale.
2. "This spike is open burning" — without BC or a validated classifier it is a *candidate*; nocturnal boundary-layer collapse mimics it city-wide.
3. "Stubble causes X % of today's PM2.5" from fire counts — counts miss > 90 % of 2024–25 fires; quote IITM DSS as a model estimate with that caveat.
4. "Our app changed behaviour/health" — only small RCTs exist (N = 28 Scotland; Bogotá self-report); none Indian or activity-aware.
5. "Indoors is safe" — NV Delhi homes show I/O ≈ 0.7–1.0.
6. "Soiling rate from a few days of yield" — SRR needs ≥ 7-day intervals and a cleaning/rain reset; haze and dust are collinear in dry smog.
7. "Hotspots are X µg/m³ above the city due to local sources" without city-median subtraction and a persistence test; annual excess is only +6–15 %; transient excess is what matters.
8. Any health-outcome number (deaths, asthma attacks) from dose — dose is defensible; individual outcome attribution is not.
9. "EPA 95th-percentile IR is typical" — EPA calls upper percentiles unrealistically high.
10. Calling October-mean AQI "typical" for Oct 8–11: early October 2024 and 2025 were Satisfactory/Moderate (PM2.5 ~55–60); the surge starts ~Oct 15–20.
